Background Note: China Tax & Legal Framework for a Manufacturing WFOE

Prepared for: APAC CFO Scope: Structural background only (no recent news). Covers a Western manufacturing group operating WFOEs in Shanghai and Wuxi. Purpose: A standing reference grid to be read alongside weekly news analysis.

1. Direct and Indirect Tax Architecture

Corporate Income Tax (CIT). The standard statutory rate is 25%. A reduced rate of 15% applies to enterprises qualifying as High and New Technology Enterprises (HNTEs) or as Advanced Technology Service Enterprises (ATSEs). Small low-profit enterprises benefit from preferential effective rates under a separate regime. Manufacturing WFOEs with significant R&D may also access super-deductions for qualifying R&D expenditure.

Value Added Tax (VAT). China replaced Business Tax with VAT in 2016. The current standard rate is 13% for most goods (including manufactured products), 9% for certain categories (transport, construction, some utilities), and 6% for many services. Export sales of goods generally qualify for zero-rating with input VAT refund, subject to the "exempt, credit, refund" mechanism. Small-scale taxpayers use a simplified 3% (occasionally 1%) levy rate.

Withholding taxes (WHT). On outbound payments: dividends, interest, and royalties are generally subject to 10% WHT, often reduced under China's bilateral tax treaties (commonly to 5% for dividends where the recipient holds ≥25%, and to 5–10% for interest/royalties). Service fees paid offshore may also attract VAT WHT at 6% (or the applicable rate) plus CIT on deemed profits in some cases.

Other taxes. Deed tax on real property acquisitions, stamp duty on contracts, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, and property tax on owned real estate. Environmental protection tax applies to certain manufacturing discharges.

2. Key Legal Framework

  • Foreign Investment Law (FIL, effective 2020). Replaced the old trio of laws governing Sino-foreign equity and cooperative JVs and WFOEs. Establishes national treatment, pre-establishment national treatment plus a negative list, and prohibits forced technology transfer. Existing WFOEs were given a transition period to align governance documents.
  • Company Law (revised, effective July 2024). Governs LLCs and JSCs, capital contribution timelines (now generally five years for subscribed capital), governance, fiduciary duties, and shareholder rights. Applies equally to domestic and foreign-invested companies.
  • Negative List. The Special Administrative Measures for Foreign Investment Access define sectors where foreign investment is prohibited or restricted. Manufacturing is largely unrestricted; a small number of sectors retain restrictions. The encouraged catalogue offers incentives in certain regions and industries.
  • Annual compliance. Key recurring obligations include: annual CIT reconciliation and filing (by May 31), monthly/quarterly VAT filings, annual industrial and commercial enterprise annual report (via the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System), foreign exchange annual reporting (SAFE), and, where applicable, customs and export tax refund filings.

3. Supervisory Authorities

  • State Taxation Administration (SAT) and local tax bureaus. Administer CIT, VAT, WHT, and other taxes. Local offices in Shanghai and Wuxi handle filings, audits, and refunds. SAT issues national circulars and interprets tax treaties.
  • Ministry of Finance (MOF). Sets tax policy, fiscal rules, and accounting standards (with the Ministry of Finance's Accounting Department).
  • State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Company registration, business licenses, annual reporting, antitrust, and product quality.
  • Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) and local counterparts. Foreign investment filing/reporting, negative list administration, and certain approvals.
  • National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC). Macroeconomic planning, foreign investment guidance, and approval/filing for certain projects and outbound investment.
  • SAFE (State Administration of Foreign Exchange). Cross-border payments, dividend repatriation, and foreign exchange registration.
  • Customs (GACC). Import/export declarations, duty assessment, and export VAT refund verification.

4. Recurring Risk Areas for a Manufacturing WFOE

  • Transfer pricing. Related-party transactions (raw materials, components, royalties, management fees) must be at arm's length. Documentation (master file, local file, CbCR) is required above thresholds. SAT scrutiny is high; APA programs exist.
  • Export VAT refunds. Zero-rating and refunds depend on proper documentation, customs classification, and supplier invoices. Mismatches or fraudulent input invoices can trigger denial, penalties, or criminal exposure.
  • Dividend repatriation. Requires sufficient after-tax profits, CIT clearance, SAFE registration, and bank documentation. WHT applies unless treaty relief is claimed. Timing and documentation are frequent audit points.
  • Data and PIPL. The Personal Information Protection Law, Data Security Law, and Cybersecurity Law impose obligations on data collection, cross-border transfer (security assessment, standard contract, or certification), and local storage for certain data. Manufacturing WFOEs with employee, customer, or supplier data are in scope.
  • Labor law. Employment contracts, social insurance, housing fund contributions, overtime, and termination rules are strictly enforced. Shanghai and Wuxi have local variations in social insurance rates and enforcement intensity.
