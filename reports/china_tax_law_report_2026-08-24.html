China WFOE – Structural Background Note for APAC CFO

Prepared for general orientation; to be read in conjunction with weekly news updates. This note reflects structural knowledge only and does not constitute legal advice.

1. Direct and Indirect Tax Architecture

A WFOE in China is subject to a layered tax system:

  • Corporate Income Tax (CIT): Standard rate is 25%. Reduced rates apply to certain qualifying entities (e.g., High and New Technology Enterprises at 15%, small low-profit enterprises at preferential rates). CIT is levied on worldwide income for resident enterprises (a WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise).
  • Value-Added Tax (VAT): Standard rate for manufacturing is 13% (goods). Services generally at 6%, with some categories (transport, construction) at 9%. VAT is a credit-method tax; input VAT on purchases is creditable against output VAT. Export of goods is generally zero-rated (with refund of input VAT), subject to documentation and compliance.
  • Withholding Taxes (WHT): On outbound payments to non-residents: dividends are subject to 10% WHT (reduced to 5% under the China–Hong Kong DTA if the Hong Kong shareholder meets substance requirements); interest and royalties generally at 10% (treaty-reduced rates may apply); service fees may be subject to WHT on deemed profit plus VAT.
  • Other taxes: Urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, stamp duty, property tax, land use tax, and social insurance contributions (employer and employee portions).

2. Key Legal Framework

  • Foreign Investment Law (FIL, effective 2020): Replaced the old three FIE laws. Establishes national treatment for foreign investors, with exceptions under the Negative List. The FIL also provides for information reporting and prohibits forced technology transfer.
  • Company Law (revised 2023, effective July 2024): Governs corporate governance, capital contribution rules (5-year paid-in deadline for registered capital), board structure, and fiduciary duties. WFOEs are typically limited liability companies.
  • Negative List (2021 edition): Restricts or prohibits foreign investment in specific sectors (e.g., certain media, telecommunications, and some manufacturing sub-sectors). Manufacturing is largely open, but check for local restrictions (e.g., rare earths, certain chemicals).
  • Annual compliance obligations: Annual report to the Market Regulation Administration (SAMR) (by June 30), annual CIT filing (by May 31), foreign investment information report (via SAMR system), social insurance annual verification, and SAFE (foreign exchange) reporting for capital and dividends.

3. Supervisory Authorities and Roles

  • State Taxation Administration (SAT) and local tax bureaus: Administer CIT, VAT, WHT, and other taxes. Local bureaus handle day-to-day filings, audits, and refunds. SAT issues policy interpretations and anti-avoidance rules.
  • Ministry of Finance (MOF): Sets tax policy direction, approves major tax legislation, and oversees accounting standards (CAS).
  • State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR): Company registration, annual reporting, and market conduct. Also oversees anti-monopoly review.
  • Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM): Foreign investment policy, negative list administration, and review of foreign investment in restricted sectors. Also handles merger control filings.
  • National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC): Oversees industrial policy, project approvals for certain investments, and pricing. Relevant for manufacturing expansion or energy-intensive projects.
  • Local tax bureaus and district-level authorities: Practical day-to-day supervision, including VAT refund processing and CIT audits.

4. Recurring Risk Areas for a Manufacturing WFOE

  • Transfer Pricing (TP): China’s TP rules (SAT Bulletin 2017-6) require arm’s-length pricing, contemporaneous documentation (local file, master file, CbC report thresholds), and annual TP reporting in the CIT return. Manufacturing WFOEs with related-party sales, IP royalties, or management fees are high-risk for adjustment.
  • Export VAT: Zero-rating requires proper export documentation (customs declarations, invoices, payment receipts). Errors lead to refund delays, penalties, or deemed sales. Export VAT refund fraud is a criminal risk; internal controls are essential.
  • Dividend Repatriation: Requires audited financials, CIT payment proof, and SAFE registration. WHT must be withheld and paid. Undistributed profits must be real; thin capitalization rules (debt-equity ratio 2:1) limit interest deductions.
  • Data and PIPL: The Personal Information Protection Law (2021) imposes strict consent, cross-border transfer, and security assessment obligations. Manufacturing WFOEs handling employee data, customer data, or IoT data must implement local storage and legal bases for transfers.
  • Labor Law: China’s Labor Contract Law mandates written contracts, social insurance contributions, and severance on termination. Collective bargaining and labor dispatch restrictions apply. Non-compete clauses require compensation. Overtime limits and work-hour rules are strictly enforced in some regions (e.g., Shanghai).
