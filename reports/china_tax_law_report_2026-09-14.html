Background Note: Tax & Legal Framework for a Manufacturing WFOE in China

Prepared for: APAC CFO Purpose: Structural background reading grid (not current news) Scope: WFOE with operations in Shanghai and Wuxi (Jiangsu Province)

1. Tax Architecture Applicable to a WFOE

Direct taxes

  • Corporate Income Tax (CIT): Standard rate 25%. Reduced rates apply to High and New Technology Enterprises (15%) and to certain encouraged projects. Small low-profit enterprises benefit from preferential regimes subject to thresholds that change over time — verify current parameters before relying on them.
  • Withholding tax (WHT) on outbound payments: Dividends, royalties, interest and certain service fees paid to offshore related parties are generally subject to 10% WHT, reducible under an applicable double tax treaty (often to 5% on dividends where shareholding and beneficial ownership conditions are met). Treaty benefits require substance and beneficial ownership analysis.

Indirect taxes

  • Value-Added Tax (VAT): Standard rate 13% for goods; 9% for certain services/transport; 6% for most modern services. A manufacturing WFOE typically deals with 13% output VAT on domestic sales and input VAT credits on purchases.
  • Export VAT refund: Exported goods generally qualify for refund of input VAT under the "exempt, credit, refund" mechanism, subject to documentation and classification.
  • Customs duties and import VAT: Payable on imported equipment and materials; import VAT is generally creditable.
  • Surtaxes: Urban maintenance and construction tax and education surcharges levied on actual VAT paid (rates vary by location).

Other

  • Individual Income Tax (IIT): Progressive rates up to 45% on employment income; withholding obligations on the employer.
  • Stamp duty on contracts and capital increases.

2. Key Legal Framework

  • Foreign Investment Law (FIL, effective 2020): Replaced the old trio of laws; establishes national treatment, negative-list management, and information reporting. Existing WFOEs retain their structure but must align governance and reporting.
  • Company Law (revised, effective 2024): Governs capital contribution timelines, registered capital, governance, and director/officer duties. The 2024 revision tightened capital contribution deadlines and liability for shareholders.
  • Negative List for Foreign Investment: Defines restricted/prohibited sectors. Manufacturing is generally outside the list, but specific sub-sectors (e.g., certain materials, defence-adjacent) may be restricted.
  • Annual compliance obligations:
  • CIT annual reconciliation (by 31 May).
  • Annual information report to SAMR (business licence annual report).
  • Foreign investment information report (MOFCOM/SAMR joint reporting).
  • IIT annual reconciliation for employees.
  • Statutory audit and, where applicable, tax audit.
  • Customs annual reporting for import/export entities.

3. Supervisory Authorities

  • State Taxation Administration (SAT) and local tax bureaus: Administer CIT, VAT, WHT, IIT; conduct audits and transfer pricing reviews. Shanghai and Wuxi have separate local bureaus under Jiangsu/Shanghai provincial coordination.
  • Ministry of Finance (MOF): Sets tax policy and accounting standards; issues circulars interpreting tax law.
  • State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR): Company registration, annual reporting, competition, and product standards.
  • Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM): Foreign investment policy, information reporting, and certain approvals.
  • National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC): Macroeconomic policy, foreign investment catalogue, and project approvals for restricted sectors.
  • Local authorities (Shanghai, Wuxi): Administer local incentives, land use, environmental and labour compliance.

4. Recurring Risk Areas for a Manufacturing WFOE

  • Transfer pricing: Related-party transactions (imports of raw materials, exports of finished goods, royalties, management fees) must be at arm's length. Documentation (master file, local file, CbCR where applicable) and benchmarking are essential. SAT scrutiny is high in manufacturing.
  • Export VAT refund: Risk of refund denial or clawback due to documentation errors, classification issues, or "fake export" allegations. Ensure invoices, customs declarations, and foreign exchange receipts align.
  • Dividend repatriation: WHT at 10% (or treaty rate) applies; requires CIT clearance, audit, and foreign exchange registration. Substance and beneficial ownership are scrutinised.
  • Data and PIPL: Cross-border transfer of employee and customer data requires security assessment, standard contracts, or certification. Manufacturing WFOEs with global ERP/HR systems are exposed.
  • Labour law: Employment contracts, social insurance, and termination rules are strictly enforced. Collective disputes and union requirements apply. Shanghai and Wuxi have local variations in social insurance contributions.
  • Foreign exchange and capital account: SAFE registration, capital contribution timelines, and profit repatriation documentation.

Note: This note is a structural background grid. Rates, thresholds, and procedural details change; verify against current law and local practice before acting. It is not legal or tax advice.
