Subject: Structural Background Note on PRC Tax, Legal & Regulatory Framework for a Manufacturing WFOE (Shanghai / Wuxi)

1. Architecture of Direct and Indirect Taxes

Corporate Income Tax (CIT)

  • Standard rate: 25% on worldwide income for a resident enterprise (WFOE is a resident enterprise if “place of effective management” is in China).
  • Reduced rates:
  • 15% for “High and New Technology Enterprises” (HNTE) – requires active R&D, IP ownership, and revenue thresholds.
  • 20% for Small Low-Profit Enterprises (SLE) on first RMB 3 million of taxable income (subject to progressive thresholds).
  • Tax year: Calendar year. Quarterly provisional filings; annual filing by 31 May.

Value-Added Tax (VAT)

  • Standard rate: 13% for manufacturing, processing, and most goods.
  • Lower rates: 9% for certain goods (e.g., books, agricultural products); 6% for services (e.g., technology, consulting).
  • Export VAT:
  • Export of manufactured goods is generally zero-rated (0% VAT) with refund of input VAT.
  • Refund rates vary by product category (often 13% or less); difference between refund rate and actual input rate is a cost.
  • Small-scale taxpayers: Simplified 3% levy (no input deduction) – not typical for a manufacturing WFOE.

Withholding Taxes (WHT)

  • Dividends: 10% WHT on profit repatriation to foreign parent (reduced to 5% if parent is a treaty-eligible resident of a jurisdiction with a tax treaty, e.g., Hong Kong, Singapore, and holds ≥25% equity).
  • Interest: 10% WHT (treaty-reduced to 7% or 5% depending on jurisdiction).
  • Royalties: 10% WHT (treaty-reduced to 6% or 5%).
  • Service fees: 6% VAT + 10% WHT on “royalty-like” payments; pure technical services may be subject to 6% VAT only if no IP transfer.

2. Key Legal Framework

Foreign Investment Law (FIL) – effective 1 January 2020

  • Replaced three old laws (Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture Law, etc.).
  • Establishes national treatment for foreign-invested enterprises (FIEs) except on the Negative List.
  • Negative List (updated annually): Prohibits or restricts foreign investment in certain sectors (e.g., rare earth mining, telecom, media). Manufacturing is generally not restricted for WFOEs, except for a few items (e.g., tobacco, certain printing).
  • Company Law (latest revision 2023, effective 1 July 2024):
  • Requires a legal representative, board of directors (or single director), and a supervisor (or audit committee).
  • Minimum registered capital abolished (except for specific industries); capital must be paid in within 5 years (new rule for limited liability companies).
  • WFOE must have a registered address in China (Shanghai or Wuxi).

Annual Compliance Obligations

  • Annual Report to SAMR (State Administration for Market Regulation) – due 30 June.
  • CIT annual filing (with audit report) – due 31 May.
  • Transfer pricing documentation (Contemporaneous Documentation) – due with CIT filing if revenue > RMB 200 million or related-party transactions exceed thresholds.
  • Foreign exchange (SAFE) reporting for capital account transactions (e.g., capital injection, dividend remittance).
  • Labor contract registration with local HR bureau; social insurance and housing fund contributions monthly.

3. Supervisory Authorities and Their Roles

| Authority | Role | |-----------|------| | SAT (State Taxation Administration) | National tax policy, CIT/VAT/WHT rules, transfer pricing guidelines. | | Local Tax Bureau (under SAT) | Day-to-day tax collection, audits, refunds, and compliance checks. | | MOF (Ministry of Finance) | Accounting standards (ASBE), tax treaty negotiation, fiscal policy. | | SAMR (State Administration for Market Regulation) | Company registration, annual report, anti-monopoly review, product quality. | | MOFCOM (Ministry of Commerce) | Foreign investment approval (now largely replaced by filing under FIL), negative list enforcement. | | NDRC (National Development and Reform Commission) | Industry policy, foreign investment guidance (catalog), large project approvals. | | SAFE (State Administration of Foreign Exchange) | Cross-border capital flows, dividend remittance, capital account conversion. | | Local HR & Social Insurance Bureaus | Labor contracts, social insurance (pension, medical, unemployment, injury, maternity), housing fund. |

4. Recurring Risk Areas for a Manufacturing WFOE

Transfer Pricing (TP)

  • Risk: Related-party transactions (e.g., management fees, royalty payments, intercompany goods) must be at arm’s length.
  • Common issues: Inadequate TP documentation, failure to file contemporaneous documentation, or excessive profit shifting to low-tax jurisdictions.
  • Penalties: 25% CIT on adjusted profit + late payment surcharge (0.05% per day).
  • Mitigation: Annual TP study, benchmarking analysis, advance pricing agreement (APA) for large transactions.

Export VAT Refund

  • Risk: Discrepancies between declared export value and actual receipts; mismatch between input VAT and refund rate.
  • Common issues: Delayed refunds, audits on “fake exports,” or incorrect classification of goods.
  • Mitigation: Maintain clean export documentation (customs declarations, invoices, payment receipts); use a reliable customs broker.

Dividend Repatriation

  • Risk: WHT rate may be challenged if beneficial ownership test fails (e.g., Hong Kong intermediary without substance).
  • Common issues: Failure to obtain tax residency certificate, or dividend paid out of reserves not properly audited.
  • Mitigation: Ensure parent entity has substance in treaty jurisdiction; obtain tax clearance before remittance.

Data / PIPL (Personal Information Protection Law)

  • Risk: Manufacturing WFOEs often collect employee data (HR, payroll, biometrics) and customer data.
  • Common issues: Cross-border transfer of personal data without security assessment (if data reaches thresholds); lack of consent mechanisms.
  • Mitigation: Conduct PIPL compliance audit; implement data localization for HR data; sign standard contractual clauses (SCCs) for cross-border transfers.

Labor Law

  • Risk: Fixed-term contracts, overtime pay, social insurance underpayment, and termination disputes.
  • Common issues: “Dispatch” workers used beyond legal limits (max 10% of workforce); failure to pay social insurance for all employees.
  • Mitigation: Use direct employment for core manufacturing roles; maintain proper overtime records; engage local labor law counsel for terminations.
