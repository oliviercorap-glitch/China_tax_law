China WFOE Background Note — Tax & Legal Framework

Prepared for: APAC CFO, Western Manufacturing Group (Shanghai & Wuxi operations) Purpose: Standing background reading grid (structural, not news-based)

1. Tax Architecture Applicable to a WFOE

Direct taxes

  • Corporate Income Tax (CIT): Standard rate 25%. Reduced rates apply to High and New Technology Enterprises (15%) and certain encouraged-catalogue projects. Small low-profit enterprises benefit from preferential effective rates. Losses may generally be carried forward 5 years (10 years for HNTE / qualifying tech-intensive enterprises).
  • Withholding tax (WHT) on outbound payments: Dividends, royalties, interest, and certain service fees paid to offshore related parties are subject to WHT, typically 10%, often reduced under China's bilateral tax treaties (commonly to 5% on dividends where ≥25% shareholding and other conditions are met; royalties/interest often 5–10%). Treaty benefits require substance and beneficial-ownership analysis.

Indirect taxes

  • Value-Added Tax (VAT): Standard rate 13% for goods (manufacturing output); 9% for certain services/transport; 6% for most modern services. Input VAT is creditable against output VAT for general taxpayers. Small-scale taxpayers use simplified rates (commonly 3%, with periodic concessions).
  • Export VAT refund: Exported goods generally qualify for refund of input VAT ("exempt, credit, refund" mechanism), subject to documentation and classification.
  • Consumption Tax: Applies only to specific product categories (e.g., certain luxury or environmentally sensitive goods); relevant if the group's product lines touch those categories.
  • Surtaxes: Urban maintenance & construction tax and education surcharges are levied on the VAT/CIT base at modest local rates.
  • Stamp Duty: Applies to listed contracts and capital instruments at low ad valorem rates.

Other

  • Individual Income Tax (IIT): Progressive rates up to 45% on employment income; withholding obligations sit with the WFOE as employer. Social insurance contributions are mandatory and locally administered.

2. Key Legal Framework

  • Foreign Investment Law (FIL, effective 2020): Governs foreign-invested enterprises, guarantees national treatment outside the negative list, and provides investment-protection principles (expropriation, profit repatriation, IP).
  • Company Law (revised, effective 2024): Governs corporate governance, capital contribution timelines, registered capital, shareholder rights, and director/officer duties. The 2024 revision tightened capital contribution deadlines and governance requirements.
  • Negative List for Foreign Investment Access: Defines sectors where foreign ownership is restricted or prohibited. Manufacturing is generally outside the list, but specific sub-sectors may be regulated.
  • Encouraged Catalogue: Determines eligibility for preferential tax and land treatment.
  • Annual compliance: Unified social credit code annual report (via SAMR), CIT annual reconciliation (by 31 May), VAT monthly/quarterly filings, IIT monthly filings, SAFE filings for cross-border flows, and statistical reporting.

3. Supervisory Authorities

| Authority | Role | |---|---| | SAT (State Taxation Administration) | National tax policy, CIT/VAT/WHT administration | | MOF (Ministry of Finance) | Tax legislation, fiscal policy | | SAMR (State Administration for Market Regulation) | Business registration, annual reporting, competition, product standards | | MOFCOM (Ministry of Commerce) | Foreign investment filing, negative-list administration | | NDRC (National Development and Reform Commission) | Investment project approval/filing, macro policy | | SAFE (State Administration of Foreign Exchange) | Cross-border payments, dividend repatriation, FX controls | | Local tax bureaus (Shanghai, Wuxi) | Day-to-day filings, audits, refund processing |

4. Recurring Risk Areas for a Manufacturing WFOE

  • Transfer pricing: Related-party transactions (royalties, management fees, intercompany sales) must be arm's-length. Documentation (master file, local file, CbCR where applicable) is expected. TP is a frequent audit focus.
  • Export VAT refund: Documentation, classification, and timing risks; refunds can be delayed or denied on technical grounds.
  • Dividend repatriation: Requires distributable profits, CIT clearance, SAFE registration, and treaty analysis for WHT reduction. FX timing and documentation matter.
  • Data / PIPL: Cross-border data transfer of employee or customer data requires security assessment, standard contracts, or certification. Manufacturing data (e.g., R&D, supply chain) may be sensitive.
  • Labor law: Employment contracts, social insurance, overtime, termination, and collective consultation rules are strictly enforced; local practice varies between Shanghai and Wuxi.
  • Customs & bonded treatment: Relevant for imported equipment, raw materials, and export processing regimes.
