China WFOE – Structural Background Note for APAC CFO

Prepared for general orientation; to be read in conjunction with weekly news updates. This note reflects standing legal and tax architecture, not current events.

1. Direct and Indirect Tax Architecture

Corporate Income Tax (CIT)
- Standard rate: 25%.
- Reduced rate: 15% for qualifying High-New Technology Enterprises (HNTEs) and certain encouraged industries in specific regions (e.g., Lingang, Hainan).
- Manufacturing WFOEs in Shanghai/Wuxi generally fall under the standard rate unless HNTE certification is obtained.

Value-Added Tax (VAT)
- Standard rate: 13% for most goods (manufacturing).
- Reduced rates: 9% (e.g., utilities, transport) and 6% (services).
- Export of goods is generally zero-rated (VAT refund mechanism applies).
- Input VAT credit system: WFOEs must match output VAT against creditable input VAT; refunds for excess credits on exports are processed via the tax bureau.

Withholding Taxes (WHT)
- Dividends to foreign parent: 10% (reduced to 5% under most tax treaties, e.g., China–Germany, China–US, subject to beneficial ownership and 25% shareholding threshold).
- Interest and royalties: 10% (treaty rates often 5–10%).
- Service fees (e.g., technical, management): treated as business profits or royalties; if no PE, generally not subject to CIT, but 6% VAT may apply on cross-border services.

Other taxes
- Land use tax, property tax, stamp duty, urban maintenance & construction tax (7% of VAT paid in urban areas), education surcharges.

2. Key Legal Framework

  • Foreign Investment Law (FIL, effective 2020): Replaced the old three FIE laws. Guarantees national treatment, intellectual property protection, and free repatriation of profits.
  • Company Law (revised 2023, effective July 2024): Governs corporate governance, capital contribution, and director duties. WFOEs are typically limited liability companies.
  • Negative List (2024 edition): Manufacturing is fully open to foreign investment (no restrictions). WFOEs in Shanghai/Wuxi are generally unrestricted.
  • Annual compliance obligations:
  • Annual audit by a Chinese CPA firm.
  • Annual report to SAMR (Market Regulation) by June 30.
  • Annual CIT filing (by May 31) and quarterly prepayments.
  • Annual transfer pricing documentation (if thresholds met).
  • Foreign exchange registration and annual FDI reporting via SAFE.

3. Supervisory Authorities and Roles

| Authority | Role | |-----------|------| | SAT (State Taxation Administration) | National tax policy; local tax bureaus (Shanghai, Wuxi) administer CIT, VAT, WHT, and audits. | | MOF (Ministry of Finance) | Tax legislation, accounting standards, and fiscal policy. | | SAMR (State Administration for Market Regulation) | Company registration, annual reporting, and market conduct. | | MOFCOM (Ministry of Commerce) | Foreign investment policy, negative list enforcement, and FDI statistics. | | NDRC (National Development and Reform Commission) | Investment project approval (for restricted/encouraged sectors), industrial policy. | | Local tax bureaus | Day-to-day tax collection, VAT refunds, and risk-based audits. |

Note: MOFCOM and NDRC have limited day-to-day role for unrestricted manufacturing WFOEs, but they matter for M&A filings and negative list compliance.

4. Recurring Risk Areas for a Manufacturing WFOE

Transfer Pricing (TP)
- China’s TP rules (SAT Bulletin 2017-6) require arm’s-length pricing for related-party transactions.
- Annual TP documentation (local file, master file, CbCR) may be required if thresholds are met (e.g., related-party revenue > RMB 200 million).
- Risk: adjustment by tax bureau, penalties, and double taxation. Mitigation: robust benchmarking, contemporaneous documentation.

Export VAT
- Zero-rating requires proper export documentation (customs declarations, invoices).
- Risk: mismatches between export data and VAT refund claims trigger audits and delays.
- Mitigation: reconcile customs data monthly, maintain clean input VAT trail.

Dividend Repatriation
- Requires board resolution, audited financials, and tax clearance.
- Risk: WHT underpayment if treaty benefits not properly claimed (beneficial ownership test).
- Mitigation: maintain substance, file treaty relief forms in advance.

Data / PIPL (Personal Information Protection Law)
- Manufacturing WFOEs handle employee and customer data. PIPL imposes cross-border transfer restrictions and local storage requirements for “important data.”
- Risk: fines up to RMB 50 million or 5% of prior-year turnover for serious violations.
- Mitigation: data mapping, privacy notices, and cross-border transfer impact assessments.

Labor Law
- Labor contracts, social insurance (mandatory), and overtime rules are strictly enforced.
- Risk: back-pay claims, social insurance arrears, and penalties for non-compliance.
- Mitigation: regular HR audits, proper work-hour records, and union/employee representative consultation for mass layoffs.

This note is a structural reference only. Weekly news updates will highlight any changes to rates, rules, or enforcement priorities.
