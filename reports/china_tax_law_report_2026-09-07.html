China WFOE – Structural Background Note for APAC CFO

Prepared for general orientation; to be read in conjunction with weekly news updates. This note reflects standing structural knowledge and does not constitute legal advice.

1. Direct and Indirect Tax Architecture

A manufacturing WFOE is subject to a layered tax system:

  • Corporate Income Tax (CIT): Standard rate is 25% on worldwide income (subject to treaty and territorial sourcing rules). Preferential rates apply: 15% for High-New Technology Enterprises (HNTE) and certain encouraged industries in specific zones (e.g., Lingang, Suzhou Industrial Park). Manufacturing WFOEs in Shanghai and Wuxi may qualify for HNTE if R&D spend and ratio thresholds are met.
  • Value-Added Tax (VAT): Standard rate is 13% for manufacturing and sale of goods. Processing, repair, and replacement services also fall under 13%. Transportation and logistics services are 9%; modern services (e.g., technical consulting) are 6%. Export of goods is generally zero-rated (with VAT refund), subject to documentation and reconciliation rules.
  • Withholding Taxes (WHT): Dividends distributed to a foreign parent are subject to 10% WHT under domestic law, reducible to 5% under most tax treaties (e.g., China–Germany, China–France, China–US) if the parent holds at least 25% (or 10% in some treaties) and meets beneficial ownership tests. Interest and royalties are subject to 10% WHT (treaty-reduced to 5–7% depending on the treaty). Technical service fees may be recharacterized as royalties if they involve transfer of know-how.
  • Other taxes: Urban maintenance and construction tax (7% of VAT paid in urban areas), education surcharges (3% + 2% local), stamp duty on contracts, and property tax (1.2% of original value or 12% of rental income). Land use tax applies to manufacturing sites.

2. Key Legal Framework

  • Foreign Investment Law (FIL, effective 1 Jan 2020): Replaced the old three FIE laws. Establishes national treatment plus negative list principle. Foreign investors enjoy equal treatment with domestic entities, except in sectors on the Negative List (where foreign ownership is restricted or prohibited). Manufacturing is generally not on the negative list; WFOEs are fully permitted.
  • Company Law (revised 2023, effective 1 July 2024): Applies to WFOEs as limited liability companies. Key changes: 5-year paid-in capital contribution deadline (for new and existing companies with transition period), stricter fiduciary duties for directors, and enhanced creditor protection. WFOEs must align their articles of association and capital contribution schedules.
  • Negative List (2024 edition): Manufacturing is fully open, except rare earths, certain minerals, and a few niche sectors. No equity caps for general manufacturing.
  • Annual compliance obligations: Annual audit by a Chinese CPA firm, filing of audited financial statements with SAMR and tax bureau, annual report to the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (by 30 June), CIT annual reconciliation filing (by 31 May), and transfer pricing documentation (if thresholds met).

3. Supervisory Authorities and Roles

  • State Taxation Administration (SAT) and local tax bureaus: Administer CIT, VAT, WHT, and all other taxes. Local bureaus handle day-to-day collection, audits, and refunds. SAT issues interpretive circulars and advance pricing arrangements (APAs).
  • Ministry of Finance (MOF): Sets tax policy, accounting standards (China GAAP), and participates in treaty negotiations. MOF and SAT jointly issue major tax regulations.
  • State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR): Registers companies, approves changes (e.g., capital increases, legal representative changes), and enforces the Company Law. Also oversees product quality and standards compliance.
  • Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM): Oversees foreign investment policy, negative list enforcement, and foreign investment information reporting (via the Foreign Investment Information Reporting System). MOFCOM approval is no longer required for non-restricted sectors, but filing is mandatory.
  • National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC): Oversees industrial policy, project approvals for certain investments (e.g., energy, infrastructure), and administers the Catalog of Encouraged Industries (relevant for preferential CIT rates and import duty exemptions).
  • Other relevant bodies: Customs (export VAT refunds, import duties), Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (labor contracts, social insurance), and Cyberspace Administration of China (data compliance).

4. Recurring Risk Areas for a Manufacturing WFOE

  • Transfer pricing (TP): China’s TP rules (SAT Circular 6/2016) require arm’s length pricing for related-party transactions. Manufacturing WFOEs are frequent audit targets, especially for low-profit margins compared to industry benchmarks. Documentation (local file, master file, CbC report) is mandatory above thresholds. Risk: TP adjustments, penalties, and interest on underpaid CIT.
  • Export VAT refunds: Zero-rating on exports requires strict matching of export documents (customs declarations, invoices, payment receipts). Mismatches or late filings can trigger refund delays or denial. Risk: cash flow strain and potential fraud allegations if documentation is incomplete.
  • Dividend repatriation: WHT at 10% (or 5% under treaty) applies only if the parent is the beneficial owner and meets substance requirements. Chinese tax authorities scrutinize shell holding structures. Risk: WHT leakage if treaty benefits are denied; also, dividends must be paid out of after-tax profits per audited accounts.
  • Data and PIPL: The Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL) imposes strict rules on cross-border transfer of personal data (including employee data). Manufacturing WFOEs transferring HR data to overseas parent systems must conduct a PIPL impact assessment and may need a standard contract or security certification. Risk: fines up to RMB 50 million or 5% of prior-year turnover for serious violations.
  • Labor law: China’s Labor Contract Law mandates written contracts, social insurance contributions (pension, medical, unemployment, injury, maternity), and severance for termination. Manufacturing WFOEs face risks around overtime caps (36 hours/month), dispatch workers (max 10% of workforce), and trade union consultation requirements. Risk: back-pay claims, administrative penalties, and reputational damage.
